## Kapittel 2

**"Tiden forsvant som sand i vinden,"** sa Ingrid Larsen, mens hun satte seg på den gamle, grå sofaen og skrattet så lite som mulig. De andre i båten – Anders Bjørnstad og politibetjenten Elin Johansen – hadde begge en tydelig, men ufullstendig forvirrende glans i øynene. 

Bjørnstad's bøygetur, den gamle, svarte verdenen i hånden, var det eneste som kunne få Anders til å se levende.  “Tiden flyter fort,” sa han med et blikk som beveget seg mellom vind og snø. "Jeg har sett en ting, eller to."

"En ting?" Elin spurte, og de små øynene på den gamle politibetjenten var av forsket interesse. 

"Å, ja," svarte Bjørnstad i et tynt, kort språk. "Det er noe i havet... Det var her det startet."  Han pekte på den grå, gamle båten, som fløy mot vinden. “Vær klar.”

En forutsigbar skygge kom til å gjøre seg bemerkbar; en usikkerhet som flyttet gjennom luften. 
"Hva med de andre?" Elin spurte, men svaringen var en stille vind i vinden som spennende. Ingrid Larsen var en gammel dame med et tydelig språk og et tydelig blikk på henne. 

“De har allerede fått sin skygge.”  Det var ikke bare wind; det var noe mer; en forvirring i de øynene, en forståelse som flyttet seg gjennom båten.

Elin kunne se at den gamle damen hadde en tanke om å gå i havnekanaler og fortvile. 

"Hvilken skygge?" Elin spurte, men svaringen var bare vinden. Ingrid Larsen hadde et blikk, en dyp sensasjon; 
“Jeg har sett de andre,” sa hun, "og de er ikke i båten." 



She stood and walked to the front of the small boat, her coat billowing slightly in the wind. The cold air whipped through her dark hair and brought a sense of unease.  Her eyes swept across the water, searching for any sign of movement or something amiss.

"Bjørnstad," Elin said, turning to him. Her voice was quiet, but there was an edge of steel in it that betrayed the undercurrent of concern. "There's no indication they were harmed. It's possible the authorities took them into custody." 


Anders Bjørnstad nodded slowly. The silence between them spoke volumes, a language all its own—a story told without words, yet carrying the weight of unspoken things.

"Maybe," he said finally, his gaze distant as if he was looking through another time or another world. "But the water's alive with secrets; I've seen them." 


Elin couldn't help but feel that something wasn’t right. The cold air hung heavy in the boat, and a strange stillness had settled upon them all. It felt as if they were being watched, studied by unseen eyes.

"We should inform the authorities," she said, breaking the tense silence. "I think there's more to this than meets the eye." 


Bjørnstad's hand reached out, his fingers drumming lightly on the side of the boat. He didn’t look at Elin or her words; he was lost in thought, a man staring into an endless sea.

"They won't understand," Bjørnstad said finally. "These things, they come and go." 


Suddenly, Ingrid Larsen began to cough violently, her eyes wide with fear. Her hand gripped the armrests of the boat like it was a life raft. “Don’t listen to them,” she gasped through the rough air. “They won't believe you.” 



Elin quickly moved closer to Ingrid and gently patted her back, trying to calm her down. "Breathe easy," she whispered, looking at Bjørnstad for guidance, her expression betraying a deep unease.


"You need air," Elin said calmly as she pushed Ingrid’s head beneath the warm wind of the boat's engine. "The authorities will help you." 



Ingrid Larsen's eyes grew cloudy. Then, as if pulled toward a dark abyss in her chest, they welled up and began to stream. “They won't understand,” she cried, her voice echoing through the small space like a fragile memory of a forgotten dream. "What was I thinking? Why did I bring them here?”



Elin watched helplessly, a knot forming in her stomach as she remembered how the man had looked at Ingrid before he took his leave. Her face turned pale as if she saw something terrible. 

“Why are you talking about them, then?” Elin asked, her voice trembling slightly with concern. “Who are these people? Why would they be a danger to us?" 


Ingrid’s gaze flickered to the horizon, as if drawn by a force unseen. "They're out there," she whispered, her eyes filled with terror. “The dead, the living… all of them.”



Then Ingrid slumped back, her head falling against the boat's floorboard. Her eyes closed; a final wave crashing over the shore of peace and reality.


Elin’s heart pounded in her chest like a trapped bird. Something was amiss, something that made the hairs on the back of her neck stand on end.  It wasn't just the dead men on the water; it was the feeling that they were all connected, bound together by some invisible thread. 



She turned to Bjørnstad, his stoic expression mirrored her own unease. “What did you see?” Elin whispered, her voice barely audible over the wind and the crashing waves.


His eyes darted around the boat; a storm brewing in their depths as if the secrets of a forgotten time were waiting to be unleashed. "The dead," he said slowly, his voice thick with an unknown sorrow. “They’re alive.” 



Suddenly, a sharp sound echoed through the boat—a cracking of ice. Elin's gaze caught it; a flash of white on the vastness of the sea. It was fleeting, yet tangible: a reflection of a broken reality in the frozen eyes of fate.  She looked at Bjørnstad. “It’s time.” 
 

The storm raged around them, a symphony of wind and rain.   But inside the small boat, something else was brewing; a sense of impending danger that felt like a slow-burning fire. It was the start, not the end; but what it promised was a story as old as time: a tale of death, mystery, and those who seek to understand the secrets of the ocean's depths.
